# Second pass: from mild to vivid

The first rewrite kept every fact but was too mild: mostly "…" and "shall" added to the old wording. The user corrected one target to show the bar:

| Input | Too mild (first pass) | The user's version: the bar |
|---|---|---|
| We're throwing Tom a farewell dinner on Friday, but he doesn't know yet. | On Friday we hold a farewell dinner for Tom. He does not know… not yet. | **We shall gather on Friday to honor Tom with a farewell feast… yet he remains unaware of what awaits him.** |

What the user's version does:
- **Reshapes the sentence:** new structure and grand verbs ("gather… to honor"), richer nouns ("feast" for dinner).
- **Grows the ending out of the input:** "yet he remains unaware of what awaits him" restates "he doesn't know yet" dramatically. It is an elaboration the input implies, not a new fact.
- **Uses one "…" at a natural turn,** not as an echo at the end.

More examples of the bar (from the user):
- "I'm really hungry. Let's get something to eat." → "Hunger has made its presence known. Let us seek sustenance."
- "I'm exhausted after today's work. I need some rest." → "The day's labors have taken their toll. I require… rest."
- "I'm stuck in a meeting. I'll call you when I'm free." → "I remain bound by this meeting. When I am free… I shall contact you."

## Rules (the validator checks these)
1. **Every fact kept.**
   - Every number (as words, same value), name, place, object, day, time, negation, condition and hedge.
   - Richer nouns are fine if the thing stays recognisable: "feast" for dinner, "sustenance" for food, "chariot" for car is too far.
2. **Speech act kept.** A question stays a question. A polite request becomes a formal one ("Will you…?", "…, if you would").
3. **No new facts.** An elaboration may only dramatise what the input already says or clearly implies ("he doesn't know yet" → "unaware of what awaits him"). No new times, people, causes, outcomes, threats or promises.
4. **No cosmic clichés:** inevitable, destiny, universe, balance, equilibrium, snap, infinity, stones, "dread it", "It always will be".
5. **No digits.** The target differs from the input.
6. **Pauses: "…" in at most 1 target in 4,** at a natural turn in the sentence. Never as an echo at the end: no "… never.", "… not yet.", "… now.", "… no more.".
7. **Serious inputs** (loss, conflict, sacrifice) get the same reshaping, sombre and weighty.
8. **Length:** one or two sentences; up to about 1.5 times the input's length.
